# V5.2: does V5 overfit for lack of data, and does fixing it lift val?

**Answer: no, a null result.** V5.2 keeps V5's network, input, vocabulary (24 major/minor chords) and
decoder, and changes only the training: first a penalty on large weights (weight decay, five
strengths), then 1,000 synthetic songs from AAM mixed into every epoch (three shares). Weight decay
never acts strongly enough to help without hurting. AAM does remove the overfitting and makes
training far less sensitive to the random seed, but every share lands within 0.3 points of V5's val
mean. V5.2 therefore keeps V5 as the frozen system, and its test numbers are V5's: **GuitarSet
72.9%, RWC-P 79.0%** (no new test run). The finding: AAM helps the pop songs (RWC-P +0.2 to +0.7)
and hurts the solo guitar takes (GuitarSet -0.6 to -1.7) in every AAM run.

| Val, best p_self per row | RWC-P | GuitarSet | mean |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| V5 (as tagged, seed 0, p_self 0.4) | 78.55 % | 75.41 % | **76.98 %** |
| V5, seed 1 (p_self 0.3) | 78.28 % | 73.86 % | 76.07 % |
| weight decay 1e-4, best of five strengths (p_self 0.4) | 79.77 % | 75.28 % | 77.52 % |
| + AAM 1:1, seed 0 (p_self 0.4) | 79.01 % | 74.83 % | 76.92 % |

| + all AAM every epoch (p_self 0.5) | 79.22 % | 74.23 % | 76.72 % |

## Question

V5's val frame accuracy peaks at epoch 5 (mean 75.1%) and then falls, while the training loss keeps
going down (0.39 at epoch 5, 0.29 at epoch 10): the network starts memorising its 120 training
songs (about 4.5 hours of audio, 163k frames) instead of learning what chords sound like. It has
145,800 weights, 110,592 of them in the final dense matrix. V5.2 asks whether this is a data problem
and tries the two standard remedies in order, keeping each one only if it beats V5 on val by more
than the run-to-run noise:

1. **Weight decay**: shrink the weights a little at every step, so the network can only keep the
   large weights the data keeps asking for.
2. **More data (AAM)**: 1,000 synthetic songs (43.4 h), about 10 times the current training audio.

## Setup

Everything is V5 (`configs/v5.yaml`, tag `v5`) except the training:

- **Run-to-run noise.** V5 retrained with a second seed scores 76.07% against 76.98% (0.91 points),
  so a change counts as a gain only if it clears that gap; for AAM, both of its seeds must beat both
  V5 seeds.
- **Weight decay** (`train_v5_2.optimizer`): AdamW instead of Adam. Adam gives every weight its own
  step size from running averages of its gradient and of its squared gradient. AdamW adds
  `w <- w - lr * weight_decay * w` at every step, outside Adam's scaling, so every decayed weight
  shrinks by the same fraction whatever its gradient. It is applied to the conv kernels and the
  dense matrix only (not biases or batch-norm gains). With weight decay 0 the code is V5's Adam
  exactly (same epoch-1 loss, 0.712). Strengths tried: 1e-4, 1e-3, 1e-2, 0.1, 1. At lr 0.001 and
  ~640 steps per epoch, 1e-2 shrinks an unsupported weight by only ~0.6% per epoch, 0.1 by ~6%.
- **AAM** (`data_aam`, `scripts/fetch_aam.py`): Artificial Audio Multitracks (Zenodo 5794629, v1.1.0,
  CC BY 4.0), songs 0001 to 1000, generated from music-theory rules and rendered with real instrument
  samples, so chords and beats are exact. Labels are one chord per beat; each beat becomes a segment,
  the last one ending at the song's 'end' mark. Only major, minor and N.C. (2.1% of beats) occur;
  N.C. gets no target, as N does in V5. Each FLAC was read out of the Zenodo zip one song at a time,
  checked against its CRC-32, cached as V5's log-CQT and deleted.
- **Mixing** (`train_v5_2.epoch_examples`): every epoch uses all 163,422 real frames plus ratio x as
  many AAM frames drawn at random without replacement, a new draw every epoch (ratio 1 and 2), or
  every AAM frame (1.62M, "all"). The prior the decoder divides by is the expected class share of
  one epoch, real and AAM weighted 1 : ratio. Early stopping looks at the real val songs only.
- The decoder, Billboard change prior and p_self grid (0.2 to 0.8) are V5's.

## How the evaluation avoids leakage

Same rule as V0 to V5.1: **test is scored once, never inspected**, and V5.2 has no test run at all.
The weights see train only (RWC-P train, GuitarSet comp train and AAM; AAM is never in val or test).
Every choice in this report uses val.

## Findings (all from val)

**1. The seed alone moves V5 by 0.9 points.** Two trainings of the same V5 differ by 0.91 in mean
WCSR (76.98 vs 76.07), mostly on GuitarSet (75.41 vs 73.86). Single-run differences below about a
point, including V5.1's, cannot be read as effects.

**2. Weight decay either does nothing or underfits.** Up to 1e-2 the weights still grow from 11 to
~29 over 10 epochs (3% smaller at most) and every run peaks at epoch 5 as V5 does. At 0.1 the
weights stay smaller (22.8) but the curve and the score are the same. At 1 the weights are pinned
near 9, the training loss stalls at 0.41 (V5: 0.29), and RWC-P falls 3.4 points. The best setting
(1e-4, +0.54) is inside the seed gap. Between "too weak to act" and "too strong to fit" there was no
setting that helped.

**3. AAM removes the overfitting, not the error.** With AAM 1:1, val frame accuracy stays flat over
12 epochs instead of falling after epoch 5, and the two seeds land 0.07 points apart (V5: 0.91).
The network is more stable, but the mean stays at V5's level (76.92 / 76.99).

**4. AAM helps pop and hurts solo guitar.** RWC-P is up in every AAM run (78.7 to 79.3 against
78.55); GuitarSet is down in every one (74.8 at 1:1 and 2:1,
74.2 with all AAM, 73.75 with only 250 songs, against 75.41). AAM's band arrangements (bass, drums, pads, melody) resemble
RWC-P's productions more than a single acoustic guitar.

**5. More AAM per epoch does not help.** 250 songs gives 76.31, 1,000 songs 76.9 to 77.0; but
raising the share to 2:1 (76.79) or all AAM (76.72) lowers it again. With all AAM, the network fits
the synthetic songs (training loss 0.10, weight norm 104 by epoch 8 against ~26) while real val
still peaks at epoch 3. AAM is easier than real music (V5 gets 87% on it untrained), so most of its
frames teach the network little about real recordings.

## Limitations

- Two seeds for V5 and for AAM 1:1, one for every other row; the noise estimate is itself rough.
- AAM's songs 1001 to 3000 and its multitracks were not used.
- One network (V5's CNN, 145,800 weights); a model with more capacity or temporal context may use
  extra data differently.

## What the next version should look at

- **V6: learned temporal context (CRNN).** Data and regularization both left V5's score unchanged,
  which points at the model: a frame classifier with 15 frames of context. V6 keeps V5's data and
  tests whether a recurrent layer over the frame sequence adds or replaces the HMM.
- **AAM for pop only, or a GuitarSet-like synthetic set.** The genre split in finding 4 suggests
  matching the synthetic data to the target style.
- **The N class with AAM.** AAM adds ~53 min of N.C. to RWC-P's 4.5 min; V5.1's N retry belongs on
  top of whichever model V6 keeps.
