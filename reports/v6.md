# V6: a recurrent layer over V5's frames (CRNN)

**Answer: the recurrent layer replaces most of what the HMM did, but it does not add to it.** V6
keeps V5's input, data, 24 major/minor classes and conv layers, and adds a GRU that reads the frames
of a song in order. On its own (most likely chord per beat, no HMM), the bidirectional CRNN is
**0.9 to 2.5 points better than V5's network alone** on val, in all four runs against both V5 seeds.
With V5's HMM decoder on top, the HMM adds only 0.4 to 0.7 points (1.7 for V5), and the full system
lands within the seed noise of V5: two seeds per setting give 76.6 to 77.4% mean WCSR against V5's
77.0 and 76.1. By the rule set in V5.2 (both seeds must beat both V5 seeds), that is not a gain on val.
The frozen V6 (bidirectional, seed 1, HMM at p_self 0.4) was scored once on test: **GuitarSet 74.7%,
RWC-P 80.0%** (V5: 72.9%, 79.0%), one seed, read after the val decision was written.

## Question

V5.2 found that neither weight decay nor ten times more (synthetic) data moves V5's val score, which
points at the model: a frame classifier that sees 1.4 s around each frame and nothing else. Every
longer-range fact (the chord held for the last bar, the change that is coming) reaches the system
only through the HMM's transition matrix, which knows how often chords change and to what, but not
what they sound like. V6 asks whether a recurrent layer over the frame sequence, learned from the
audio, adds to the HMM or replaces it.

Three comparisons answer it, each on two seeds:

1. **Network alone**, V6 against V5: does the GRU make the frame posteriors themselves better?
2. **Network + HMM**, V6 against V5: does the better network still gain once the HMM smooths both?
3. **Causal against bidirectional**: how much of the gain needs the future? A real-time tracker
   (the project's goal) can only use the past.

## Setup

Everything is V5 (`configs/v5.yaml`, tag `v5`) except the acoustic model and its training input:

    input     V5's log-CQT (data/processed/v5_logcqt/, reused); a training example is an excerpt of
              128 consecutive frames (11.9 s) plus 7 frames of context on each side (data_v6.excerpt)
    shift     one random k in -5..+6 per excerpt, for all its frames: a progression keeps its
              intervals (C -> G -> Am becomes D -> A -> Bm), so the GRU still sees orders music uses
    tiling    every song cut into back-to-back excerpts from a random offset, a new offset each epoch:
              each frame with a target is in one excerpt per epoch, as each frame was once in V5
    front     V5's conv blocks A, B, C on every frame's own 15-frame window (the strip is unfolded,
              so frame t sees exactly V5's patch), mean over time      -> 64 x 72 per frame
    embed     conv 1 x 1, 64 -> 8 channels (keeps the 72 semitone rows), dense 576 -> 64, ReLU
    GRU       hidden 64; bidirectional (128 out) or causal (64 out)
    out       dropout 0.5, dense -> 24 logits per frame
    size      125,648 weights bidirectional, 99,152 causal (V5: 145,800, 110,616 of them in its dense layer)
    training  cross-entropy over every frame with a target, Adam lr 1e-3, 8 excerpts per step
              (1,024 frames), early stopping on the mean val frame accuracy (patience 5), seeds 0 and 1
    inference a whole song is one excerpt: the conv runs in chunks, the GRU reads the song in one pass
    decoders  argmax per beat (the network alone), and V5's: frame posteriors -> beats (geometric
              mean) -> Viterbi with A = p_self I + (1 - p_self) Q, divided by the training prior

Symbols as in reports/v5.md: A is the 24 x 24 transition matrix, p_self the probability of staying on
the same chord one more beat, Q V3's Billboard change prior.

- **Why the 1 x 1 convolution.** V5's dense layer reads all 4,608 numbers of a frame. Feeding that to
  a GRU would put most of the model in one matrix again; mixing 64 channels down to 8 per semitone row
  keeps where a pattern is (root and octave) with 576 inputs. The CRNN ends up smaller than V5.
- **Same front end, so any change is the GRU's.** The tests check that each frame's window is
  `data_v5.patch` of that frame and that the conv layers are V5's (`tests/test_model_v6.py`).
- **Run-to-run noise.** V5.2 measured 0.91 points between two V5 seeds. A V6 setting counts as a gain
  only if both its seeds beat both V5 seeds.
- **Corrections.** At most two, one change at a time, each with a reason and a val rerun. One was made
  (batch size, below); no grid search.

## How the evaluation avoids leakage

Same rule as V0 to V5.2: **test is scored once, never inspected.** The weights see RWC-P train and
GuitarSet comp train only. Every choice (architecture, batch size, bidirectional or causal, decoder,
seed, p_self) was made on val. The val decision in this report was written before the test run, which
used `run_v6.py --split test --freeze`, and only its summary numbers were read.

## Findings (all from val)

**1. The GRU makes the network better on its own.** The bidirectional CRNN's argmax per beat scores
76.2 to 76.8% in all four runs, against 75.3 and 74.4 for V5's network alone: every V6 run beats both
V5 seeds, by 0.9 to 2.5 points. Its frame accuracy at the best epoch is 76.3 to 77.3% (V5: 75.1 and
74.1). It also changes chord less often without an HMM: 32 to 36 changes per minute against V5's 37.

**2. The HMM then adds little, and the full system stays at V5's level.** The HMM adds 1.65 and 1.70
points to V5 but only 0.43 and 0.72 to the bidirectional CRNN (0.54 and 0.37 at batch 2), and on RWC-P
it adds nothing or costs a little (78.66 alone, 78.23 with the HMM). The GRU has already learned the
smoothing and part of what the change prior knew. The combined scores, 76.6 to 77.4%, overlap V5's
76.1 to 77.0: one of the two seeds is below V5 seed 0 in both bidirectional settings. The GuitarSet
styles show the same picture: BN, Jazz and Rock gain 1 to 5 points, SS stays at 89, and Funk, the
weakest style, does not improve (45 to 47 against 47 to 51).

**3. The GRU does use the context.** Restarted every frame, the bidirectional model drops to 73.3%
frame accuracy; restarted every 16 frames (1.5 s), 75.9%; reading the whole song, 77.3%. Most of the
gain is within the first 64 frames (6 s), the rest comes from the song. Reading the whole song at
inference while training on 12 s excerpts does no harm (chunks of 128 give 76.95%, the whole song
77.26%).

**4. Causal is about as good as bidirectional once the HMM is on.** Without the HMM, the causal model
is about a point behind (75.3 against 76.5% mean of the two seeds), and its front end alone is weaker
(70.6 against 73.3%: trained to lean on the past, it relies less on its own window). With the HMM it
lands at 76.09 and 77.06, inside the bidirectional range: the HMM's Viterbi pass looks both ways and
gives back what the GRU could not see. The HMM adds 1.0 to 1.5 points to the causal model.

**5. Seeds still move the score by about a point.** Bidirectional seeds differ by 0.80 (batch 8) and
0.76 (batch 2), causal by 0.97; best epochs range from 7 to 24 because the val curve moves 1 to 2
points between epochs and early stopping catches different peaks. Single-run differences below a
point mean nothing here.

**Freeze.** Bidirectional (finding 4 gives causal no edge, and V6's question is the GRU itself),
batch 8 (correction 1 changed nothing), the HMM (it is ahead of argmax in every run), seed 1 (the
better of the two batch-8 seeds) and its p_self 0.4. Because no V6 setting met the two-seed rule, V6 is
reported as a null result for the full system: V5 and V6 are tied on val.

## Test

Run once, after the freeze, with `results/v6/bidir_seed1/model.pt` and p_self 0.4.

| | WCSR | changes / min | scored s | of which reduced s | excluded s |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| GuitarSet (60 takes) | **74.69 %** | 24.5 | 1,723.3 | 306.3 | 153.1 |
| RWC-P (20 songs) | **80.00 %** | 28.1 | 4,783.4 | 1,851.2 | 248.1 |

The scored seconds are V5's on both sets, so the comparison is direct: +1.8 points on GuitarSet and
+1.05 on RWC-P. This is one model, the better of two seeds on val; on val the two seeds of each
system are a point apart, so these test differences are within what a reseed can move. The val-test
gap on GuitarSet is 1.9 points (V5: 2.5); RWC-P test is 1.8 points above val (V5: 0.4).

## Limitations

- **Two seeds per setting.** The noise estimate rests on two runs each, and the frozen model was the
  better seed on val, which flatters both its val and (to a lesser degree) its test score.
- **Same 24 classes and data as V5.** No N class, sevenths reduced to triads, 120 training songs.
- **The causal model is not a real-time system yet.** Its GRU only uses the past, but the beat
  tracker and Viterbi both read the whole song. A real-time tracker needs a causal decoder (forward
  filtering, or a fixed-lag Viterbi) and causal beats.
- **Not bit-exact on the Mac's GPU**, as in V5.

## What the next version should look at

- **V7: real time.** The causal CRNN is within the noise of the bidirectional one once a decoder that
  looks both ways is added. The next question is what is lost when the decoder is causal too: the
  causal CRNN's argmax per frame, forward filtering with V5's transition matrix, and a fixed-lag
  Viterbi (0.5 to 2 s of look-ahead), all on V6's causal model.
- **A decoder that knows the CRNN's posteriors are already smooth.** The HMM adds 0.4 to 0.7 points to
  the bidirectional model and costs a little on RWC-P; dividing by the prior and pooling per beat
  may double-count what the GRU already does. Frame-level decoding (no beats) is the obvious ablation,
  since it also takes beat-tracking errors (Funk) out of the loop.
- **Funk** still does not improve with temporal context (45 to 47%): its errors are not smoothing
  errors.
- **The N class and sevenths** (V5.1's and V5's open items) on top of the CRNN.
