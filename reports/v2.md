# V2: do better chroma features help the triad templates?

**Answer: yes, by 4.2 points on test, the first real gain since V0's Viterbi.** V2 keeps V1's
beat pooling, templates and decoder, and changes the chroma in three steps, each chosen alone on
val: remove the stationary floor of the low CQT bins (the guitar body resonance), compress the
CQT with a log, and add a bass chroma to the template score. On the untouched test split the HMM
goes from 53.3% (V1) to 57.5% WCSR, and the per-beat argmax from 48.8% to 50.1%.

| Test split, run once (60 takes) | V1 | V2 | changes / min, V1 → V2 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Argmax per beat (framewise) | 48.8 % | 50.1 % | 61 → 63 |
| **Viterbi** (p_self chosen on val) | 53.3 % (0.05) | **57.5 %** (0.06) | 29 → 25 |

## Question

V1's val analysis left two feature problems. A fixed peak at about 106 Hz, between G#2 and A2,
holds up to 45% of the CQT energy of a Funk take whatever chord is played (a guitar body
resonance excited by muted strums), and it pushes every chord towards G# and A. And the triad
templates cannot tell apart chords that share two notes: F A in the treble is D:min or F:maj, and
an A#:maj7 voiced F A D A# looks like D:min. V2 asks whether three standard feature fixes close
part of the gap to the note oracle (perfect chroma from the transcribed notes, same templates and
Viterbi: 70.8% on val).

## Setup

Everything is V1 except the chroma. V2 computes the CQT magnitude itself (the same CQT
`chroma_cqt` uses: 7 octaves from C1, 36 bins per octave, tuning estimated per file), changes it,
and folds it back with `chroma_cqt(C=...)`. With every V2 step off, this gives the V1 chroma
(max difference 3e-7 on val; a slow test checks it on a train take).

| Step | What it does | Setting (chosen on val) |
|---|---|---|
| Floor | per CQT bin, subtract a percentile of that bin over the whole take: `max(C - floor, 0)` | median, only bins below 200 Hz |
| Compression | divide by the take's level (99th percentile of C), then `log(1 + gamma C)` | gamma 10 |
| Threshold | `chroma_cqt`'s absolute threshold, applied before normalization | removed (V1: 0.25) |
| Bass chroma | fold only the bins from E1 (41 Hz) to B3 (247 Hz), max-normalize, mean per beat | |
| Emission | `template . treble + w * (root . bass)`, softmax, beta 1; N gets `w * 0.25 * sum(bass)` | w 0.2 |
| Decoder | Viterbi per beat, `transition_loop(25, p_self)` | p_self 0.06 |
| Code | `configs/v2.yaml`, `src/chordtracker/features_v2.py`, `src/chordtracker/bass.py`, `scripts/run_v2.py` | |

Why each step should help:

- **The floor targets what is always there.** A bin's median over a take is the level it stays
  above for half of the frames. The resonance is in nearly every frame, so it is its own median and
  is removed; a chord note is in the median only if it sounds in that bin for more than half of
  the take. That limit is real (a pedal note or a one-chord song would lose its note), so the
  ablation measures it with GuitarSet's note transcription.
- **The log** stops loud low notes from outweighing the quieter chord tones in the fold.
- **The bass chroma covers the low register of any band**, not only the guitar's: a bass guitar's
  low E is E1 (41 Hz), the guitar's is E2 (82 Hz). Its root picks between triads that share two notes.

## How the evaluation avoids leakage

Same rule as V0 and V1: **test is scored, never inspected.** Every setting above was chosen on
val, one step at a time, with p_self re-swept at every step. Test was
run once, with the frozen code and p_self 0.06, and only its pooled numbers and the count of
takes where Viterbi beats framewise appear here.

## Choices on val

Each row adds one step to the row above; p_self is re-swept every time.

| Step (val, 60 takes) | WCSR | p_self | changes / min | takes better / worse than the row above |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| V1 | 61.3 % | 0.05 | 29 | |
| + floor: median, below 200 Hz | 63.7 % | 0.05 | 30 | 36 / 14 |
| + take level, log(1 + 10 C), no threshold | 65.1 % | 0.055 | 28 | 34 / 25 |
| **+ bass chroma, w 0.2** | **67.3 %** | 0.06 | 27 | 34 / 13 |
| note oracle (V1 report) | 70.8 % | | | |

**Floor.** Variants against V1 on val ("note energy kept": CQT energy in the bins of the
transcribed notes, partials 1 to 4, while they sound):

| Floor | WCSR | better / worse | note energy kept | other energy kept |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| median, every bin | 63.3 % | 35 / 18 | 92.9 % | 65.1 % |
| half the median | 62.5 % | 28 / 15 | 96.3 % | 79.2 % |
| 20th percentile | 62.6 % | 28 / 11 | 98.6 % | 86.0 % |
| **median, below 200 Hz** | **63.7 %** | 36 / 14 | 97.0 % | 72.9 % |
| 20th percentile, below 200 Hz | 62.8 % | 30 / 7 | 99.2 % | 88.2 % |
| whitening (divide by the bin mean) | 28.7 % | 2 / 58 | | |

Over every bin, the median removes 7% of the note energy and gives little back above 200 Hz;
below 200 Hz only, it removes 3% of the notes and 27% of the rest. The 20th percentile is the
safest, and was checked again in the full chain: about 2 points below the median at every bass weight.

**Compression.** gamma 1 and 10 give 64.6 to 65.1%; 100 gives 63.5 to 63.9%; 1000 gives 59.5 to
61.6% (the chroma becomes flat and noise bins compete). V1's threshold of 0.25 is an absolute
value applied before normalization: it zeroes about 60% of the folded values on V1, and almost
none after the log, so it was tested on and off; with the log it makes no difference, and V2
drops it because it depends on the recording level. Dividing by the take's level makes gamma
level-independent too; on GuitarSet, recorded at one level, it scores the same as without.

**Bass weight.** 66.2% at w 0.1, 67.0 to 67.3% from 0.15 to 0.35, 65.8% at 0.5, 61.9% at 1, 56.1% at 2.

## Test, run once

| System | test WCSR | changes / min | takes improved | reduced | excluded |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| framewise (V2 features, beats) | 50.1 % | 63 |  | 17.8 % | 8.2 % |
| Viterbi 0.06 | 57.5 % | 25 | 49 / 60 | 17.8 % | 8.2 % |

"Takes improved" counts the test takes where Viterbi beats framewise, as in V0 and V1. Compared
with V1 on test: framewise +1.3 points, Viterbi +4.2 points. On val the same comparison gives +5.3
and +5.9. This is reported, not investigated.

## Findings (all from val)

**1. The resonance was costing points everywhere, not only in Funk.** The low-band floor alone
gives +2.4 points (BN 54.1 → 58.8, Funk 31.7 → 35.1, Rock 75.9 → 78.1, SS 84.0 → 85.8). Jazz
does not move (48.8 → 48.5): its errors come from the voicings, not the resonance.

**2. Above 200 Hz the floor removes notes, not noise.** Restricting the median to the low bins
scores higher and keeps more of the transcribed notes (97.0% vs 92.9%). The guitar's chord tones
live above 200 Hz and stay in many frames of a take, so a full-range median eats them.

**3. The bass chroma fixes what the triads could not.** It brings Jazz from 50.8 to 56.4 and BN from
60.4 to 66.5, the styles with extended and rootless voicings, and leaves Rock, Funk and SS within
a point. A small weight is enough: the template score sums three chroma values and the bass adds
one, so w 0.2 breaks near-ties. From w 0.5 the bass overrides the treble and inversions or passing
bass notes become wrong roots (Rock 80.3 → 72.6 at w 1).

**4. p_self moves to 0.06.** The bass term adds evidence to each beat, so the transition cost can
be a little higher than V1's 0.05 (V1 finding 2: the useful p_self range follows the evidence per step).

**5. The remaining gap is the templates and the beats.** V2 closes 6.0 of the 9.5 points between
V1 and the note oracle on val. Funk is still lowest (33.8%, oracle 48.8%): its muted, syncopated
chords defeat both the beat tracker and the triad templates.

## Limitations

- **The val–test gap grew.** V1 lost 8.0 points from val to test, V2 loses 9.8 (67.3% → 57.5%). Three
  steps and three settings were chosen on the same 60 val takes, so part of the val gain is fit to
  val. The test gain over V1 (+4.2) is the honest number.
- **No bass guitar in GuitarSet.** The bass range E1..B3 is chosen for general music, but val only
  tests the guitar's own low strings. With a real bass part, louder and more reliable, the best
  weight may differ.
- **The floor is per take.** A take-level median needs the whole take; a live version (V7) needs a
  running percentile over the last seconds instead.
- **The 200 Hz edge is set by a guitar body.** In a full mix the low band also holds bass and kick
  drum, and the edge would need a second look.
- **Same vocabulary as V0:** triads only, binary templates, beta 1.

## What the next version should look at

- **Templates and emission** (seventh chords, or learned emissions, V4): the note oracle shows that
  even perfect notes give 70.8% with triad templates, and V2 is within 3.5 points of it on val.
- **Beat tracking for syncopated styles:** annotated beats gave +1.7 points on V1 val, and Funk is
  still the weakest style.
- Fewer knobs per version, or a second validation pass, to keep the val–test gap from growing.

## Reproduce

```bash
python scripts/run_v2.py --split val                       # sweep p_self with the V2 features (0.06)
python scripts/run_v2.py --split test --p-self 0.06        # once
```

The per-step ablations above were run with one-off scripts on cached val CQTs, outside the repo;
`configs/v2.yaml` records each choice next to its val numbers.
