# V9: a class-weighted loss for the rare chord qualities

## Question

V8's 60-class model reads most 7 chords as maj: on validation, 7 recall is 21 to 39 % for the network
alone, and 79 to 92 % of the missed 7 frames go to maj. maj is 60 % of the training frames, min 18 %,
7 9 %, min7 9 % and maj7 4.5 %. V9 asks: **with V8's model, data and decoders
unchanged, does weighting the rare qualities in the training loss give more correct sevenths without
losing major/minor accuracy?**

## Summary of the results

No, a null result. On validation (three sets, never pooled):

1. **The weights do what they target.** 7 recall rises at every decoder point, for example for the
   network alone on ChoCo from 21.0 % (V8) to 35.2 % (alpha 0.25) and 55.2 % (alpha 0.5), and the share
   of frames read as maj falls toward the reference.
2. **The scores fall.** maj recall drops by more than 7 recall gains in frames. Sevenths goes down on
   every set at every decoder setting, and majmin on the mean of the three sets at every point. The best sevenths on the mean of the three sets is 72.99 % for
   V8, 71.06 % for alpha 0.25 and 70.24 % for alpha 0.5. That is a loss of 1.9 and 2.8 points, larger
   than the 0.6-point gap between V8's two seeds.
3. **The weights and the decoder prior push the same way.** V8 divides the network's posterior by
   prior^0.25 to give the rare classes a chance. With weighted training, that division over-predicts
   them: at prior power 0.5 and alpha 0.5 the HMM reads 7 on 19.1 % of RWC-P frames (reference 6.3 %).
   The weighted models score best at a lower prior power, but still below V8.

V8 stays the frozen model. `train.class_weight_alpha` stays at its default 0 (V8's unweighted loss),
and **test was not run**: no V9 setting beat V8 on validation.

## Setup

Everything is V8's frozen setting (vocabulary A: maj, min, 7, maj7, min7 on 12 roots; the causal CRNN
on the C2 grid with input look-ahead 2; RWC-P, GuitarSet and the ChoCo export; seed 1; the online
decoders with the Billboard change counts) except the training loss:

    loss = (1 / n) * sum over frames t with a target of  w[y_t] * CE(logits_t, y_t)
    w[c] = max(f_c, floor) ** -alpha / sum_c' f_c' * max(f_c', floor) ** -alpha

Symbols: y_t is the target class of frame t, CE the cross-entropy, and n the number of frames with a
target in the batch. f_c is the share of training frames of class c, counted in all 12 transpositions
(`transitions_v8.training_prior`, the same prior the decoder divides by). floor = 1e-4.

- **One weight per quality.** Every training excerpt is shifted by a random one of the 12 semitone
  shifts, so the loss sees each root of a quality equally often. The pooled share is the frequency the
  loss actually meets. Weighting by the annotated roots would weight a distribution the network is
  never trained on.
- **Mean weight 1.** The scaling makes the frame-weighted mean of w over the training frames exactly 1.
  The loss divides by n, not by the summed weights of the batch (which is what PyTorch's weighted mean
  does), so the overall loss scale and the learning rate keep V8's meaning.
- **alpha 0 is V8.** At alpha 0 every weight is 1 and training takes the unweighted code path,
  unchanged (a test checks it). alpha 0.5 is weighting by 1 / sqrt(frequency); alpha 0.25 is half as
  strong in the exponent.
- **The floor** bounds the weight of a class that never occurs in training; in vocabulary A every class
  occurs, so it is not reached.
- **Early stopping is unchanged:** mean validation frame accuracy over the three sets, patience 5,
  unweighted.

The weights per quality, on V8's training frames:

| alpha | maj | min | 7 | maj7 | min7 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 (V8) | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| 0.25 | 0.82 | 1.12 | 1.32 | 1.57 | 1.33 |
| 0.5 | 0.64 | 1.18 | 1.66 | 2.34 | 1.68 |

Each weighted model was decoded at prior power 0, 0.25 (V8's choice) and 0.5, since a weighted network
already favours the rare classes and may need less of the prior division. The V8 model was decoded at
the same three powers. The scores are V8's:
- **Aligned WCSR:** the reference is shifted by the system's total look-ahead.
- **majmin:** mir_eval's scoring on maj and min triads.
- **sevenths:** mir_eval's MIREX sevenths. For example, a C:7 frame read as C:maj counts as right in
  majmin and wrong in sevenths.

Code: `train_v8.class_weights`, `train_v7_2.frame_loss`, and `train.class_weight_alpha` /
`class_weight_floor` in `configs/v8.yaml` (`--class-weight-alpha` in `scripts/run_v8.py`). Runs in
`results/v9/A_a025_seed1` and `results/v9/A_a050_seed1`; the V8 model's new prior-power-0 decode is in
`results/v9/v8_A_seed1/val_pp0`.

## How the evaluation avoids leakage

Same rule as every version: **test is scored, never inspected.**
- **Choices on validation only:** the weighting, both alphas, the prior powers and the decoder points
  were all compared on the validation splits of RWC-P (20 songs), GuitarSet (60 takes) and ChoCo (47
  recordings).
- **Weights from the training targets only.**
- **Fixed decoder points:** network alone, lag 2 at p_self 0.5, lag 4 at 0.8 and lag 8 at 0.98, as
  frozen in V8. No p_self was re-tuned for the weighted models, so they are compared at the same
  settings.

Because no V9 setting beat V8 on validation, V9 keeps V8, and **test was not run again**.

## Findings (all from validation)

**1. More 7s found, more maj chords lost.** maj is 59 to 72 % of the reference frames on the
three sets, 7 is 6 to 10 %. At alpha 0.5 the network alone gains 19.8 points of 7 recall on RWC-P
(39.1 to 58.9 %) and loses 9.9 points of maj recall (90.1 to 80.2 %). maj has about ten times as many
frames, so the lost maj frames outnumber the gained 7 frames several times over. A maj frame read as 7
is also wrong in sevenths, so sevenths falls even where 7 recall rises the most.

**2. The errors move, they do not go away.** At each model's best prior power, the weighted network
still sends most missed 7 frames to maj on RWC-P and ChoCo (78 to 93 % at lag 8). On GuitarSet more of
them go to min and min7 instead (alpha 0.5 at lag 8: 34 % to min and 13 % to min7, against 16 % and
8 % for V8). The extra 7 predictions are mostly on
frames that are not 7: at alpha 0.5, prior power 0.25 and lag 8, RWC-P reads 7 on 13.8 % of frames
against 6.3 % in the reference, while 7 recall is 67.6 %. The weighting moves the decision threshold
between maj and 7 without making the two easier to tell apart.

**3. Loss weights and the prior division are the same lever.** Dividing the posterior by prior^p
multiplies each class's score by prior^-p. Weighting the loss by f^-alpha moves the trained network's
log-odds roughly the same way. The two add up:
- **alpha 0.25:** prior power 0.25 is still best on the mean.
- **alpha 0.5:** prior power 0 is best. At power 0.5 sevenths falls to 63 to 65 %, and RWC-P reads 7 on
  19.1 % of frames.

More 7 recall can be had from either lever, but no combination of the two reaches V8's 72.99 %
sevenths on the mean.

**4. majmin falls too.** On the mean it drops 0.7 to 1.7 points at matched points, much of it through
min recall (RWC-P, network alone: 70.0 % for V8, 66.0 % at alpha 0.25, 65.2 % at alpha 0.5). At alpha
0.25 more min frames are read as maj (1,245 RWC-P frames against V8's 1,015). At alpha 0.5 more are read
as min7 (757 against 502), which majmin counts as right but sevenths does not. The one place a weighted
model is not below V8 is majmin on ChoCo at prior power 0.5: alpha 0.25 is level or up to 0.2 points
higher there, with lower sevenths.

**5. The weighted runs stop earlier.** Best epoch 7 and 9, against 14 for V8, on the same unweighted
frame-accuracy criterion. The criterion favours the unweighted model. A weighted criterion such as
sevenths or mean per-quality recall could keep a weighted model training longer, but it would also
change what is being optimised: V9 kept V8's criterion so that only the loss changes.

## Limitations

- **One seed per alpha.** V8's two seeds differ by 0.6 points of best mean sevenths (72.99 / 72.38 %),
  less than the 1.9 and 2.8 points V9 loses, so the direction is clear. The size is from one run each.
- **Two strengths only.** alpha below 0.25 (0.1, for example) was not tried. It would sit between V8
  and alpha 0.25, and finding 3 suggests it would mostly replace part of the prior division.
- **Weights per quality, not per root and quality,** for the reason in Setup. maj6, sus4 and dominant 9
  (vocabularies B and B9) were not retrained with weights. They are rarer still and were the other
  target of the idea.
- **Same early-stopping criterion and decoder points as V8.** p_self was not re-tuned for the weighted
  models.
- **Licence.** The two V9 models are trained on V8's data and carry V8's terms: research and
  non-commercial use only.

## What the next version should look at

In order of expected value:

1. **The play-along check with V8's labels:** the 60-class model and prior power 0.25 in the live
   chain, by ear, on songs with many 7ths.
2. **The tuning estimate of the live path.** The live and training features still estimate the tuning
   on 24 bins, which wraps at +/-25 cents and can put an off-tune recording a semitone away (open
   issue #11).
3. **An N class** on the 60-class model: the system always names a chord, also in silence.

Finding 2 suggests that telling 7 from maj needs better evidence for the minor seventh rather than a
different loss: reweighting moves the threshold between the two classes but does not separate them.

PYTHONPATH=src python scripts/run_v8.py --vocab A --train --seed 1 --class-weight-alpha 0.25 --out results/v9/A_a025_seed1
