# V3: does a learned chord-change prior beat the flat self-loop?

**Answer: no, a null result.** V3 replaces the decoder's flat `transition_loop(25, p_self)` with a
prior on *where* a chord goes when it changes, counted on chord labels and shared across keys. On
val, the best prior learned from GuitarSet's train lead sheets scores 67.67% and the best one from
730 Billboard songs 67.47%, against V2's 67.28%; both improve as many takes as they harm. A minimum
chord duration is worse at every length. V3 therefore keeps the V2 decoder, and its test number is
V2's: **57.5% WCSR** (no new test run). The finding is the reason: with template evidence, a prior
cannot be used at its true strength, which moves the question to V4's learned, calibrated emissions.

| Val split (60 takes), best p_self per row | WCSR | changes / min | takes better / worse than V2 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| V2 decoder: transition_loop, p_self 0.06 | 67.28 % | 27.0 | |
| + change prior from train labels (tau 0.25, p_self 0.12) | 67.67 % | 27.5 | 27 / 28 |
| + change prior from Billboard labels (tau 0.25, p_self 0.10) | 67.47 % | 25.7 | 22 / 22 |
| + minimum duration 2 beats (uniform prior, p_self 0.20) | 66.43 % | 30.3 | 20 / 21 |
| change prior fitted on the val labels themselves (ceiling only) | 71.42 % | 26.1 | 32 / 17 |

## Question

Since V0 the decoder has assumed that a changing chord is equally likely to go to any of the 24 other
states. Real progressions are not like that: C:maj goes to G:maj or F:maj far more often than to
C#:min. The roadmap's V3 asks whether a realistic prior of chord changes beats the flat one, with
the features, beats and emission held at V2.

## Setup

Everything is V2 (`configs/v2.yaml`) except the transition matrix:

    A[i, i] = p_self
    A[i, j] = (1 - p_self) * Q[i, j]                   for j != i
    Q[i, j] proportional to (count[q_i, q_j, (r_j - r_i) mod 12] + alpha) ** tau

Symbols: A is the 25 x 25 transition matrix Viterbi uses, i and j are chord states, r is a root
(0 = C, ..., 11 = B) and q a quality (maj or min). count is the number of chord changes in the
label data from quality q_i to quality q_j with that root interval. It is **transposition-invariant**:
C -> G and D -> A are both "maj -> maj, up 7 semitones" and share one count, so the prior holds in
every key. alpha = 1 is Laplace smoothing (an unseen move counts as seen once, so nothing is
forbidden). tau weights the prior: tau = 0 makes every off-diagonal Q equal to 1/24, which is exactly
`transition_loop`, the V2 decoder (a test checks it), and tau = 1 uses the smoothed frequencies.
Moves into and out of N are flat (GuitarSet has no N in its labels).

p_self stays a val knob rather than being learned. On train's annotated beats a chord lasts 6.4
beats, which would mean p_self = 1 - 1/6.4 = 0.84; V1 and V2 chose 0.05 to 0.06 because a beat mean
scored with beta 1 carries little evidence, so the transition costs must be small (V1 finding 2).
V3 learns where a chord goes, not how long it stays.

| Source of the counts | songs | chord changes | change types seen (of 47) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| GuitarSet train lead sheets (progression 1 of each style) | 5 progressions | 420 | 7 |
| McGill Billboard, from ChoCo v1.0.0 (labels only, no audio) | 730 | 58,794 | 46 |

The minimum-duration variant gives every chord k sub-states that must be passed in order, so it lasts
at least k beats; only the last one has the self-loop.

## How the evaluation avoids leakage

Same rule as V0 to V2: **test is scored, never inspected.** The split is by progression (train = 1,
val = 3, test = 2 of each style), so a prior counted on train is tested on progressions it has never
seen. Billboard shares no song with GuitarSet. Every setting was compared on val only, with p_self
re-swept for every prior and every tau. The "val labels" row fits the prior on the very labels it is
scored against; it measures how much any change prior could add here and is never a candidate.
Because no V3 variant beat V2 on val, V3 is V2's decoder and **test was not run again**.

## Findings (all from val)

**1. A prior is worth about 4 points here, but not one learned from five lead sheets.** The
val-label ceiling reaches 71.42%, so progressions do carry information the flat loop ignores. The
train prior gets part of it where val moves like train (Rock +8.5, BN +4.2) and loses it where it does
not: train has no "min -> maj, up 5" (Dm -> G, the jazz ii -> V), which is 11% of val's changes, and
Jazz drops from 56.4 to 48.0 at tau 0.25 and to 35.6 at tau 1.

**2. A large corpus fixes coverage but not fit.** Billboard sees 46 of the 47 change types, and Jazz
recovers (57.9 at tau 0.25), but GuitarSet's lead sheets are not pop songs, so Rock and BN lose the
gains train gave them. Pooled, it is a tie.

**3. At full strength any learned prior overrides the evidence.** In the log domain Viterbi adds the
beat's template score (beta 1) and, at each change, log Q. Two plausible chords share two of their
three notes, so their beat scores differ by about 1, about one nat (natural-log unit) of evidence per
beat. Billboard's commonest move has Q = 0.20 and a rare one about 0.001: a ratio of 200, log 200 =
5.3 nats. One transition then outweighs about five beats of evidence and bends the chords towards
pop moves (tau 1: 60.66%). tau = 0.25 shrinks that to 1.3 nats, which is why only weak priors are
harmless. It is the same calibration problem that keeps p_self at 0.06: the template posteriors are
softmaxed fit scores, not probabilities, so no prior can be used at its true weight.

**4. p_self rises as the prior sharpens.** With a flat Q every move costs (1 - p_self)/24. A sharp Q
gives the top move up to 0.46 (1 - p_self): at p_self 0.06 changing to it (0.43) beats staying (0.06),
and the path flickers at 117 changes per minute. The val sweep moves p_self to 0.2 to 0.4 to keep
the self-loop competitive.

**5. Durations are not the problem.** V2 already decodes 27.0 changes per minute against the lead
sheets' 27.5, so there is little flicker to remove. Val's chords last 3 to 5 annotated beats (95%;
28% last 3), and the detected beats are sometimes at half tempo, so a real chord can be one or two
decoder steps long, which a minimum duration forbids.

**6. No-chord cannot be measured on GuitarSet.** Its labels contain no N, and V2 decodes N on no beat
of any val take. The roadmap's "real no-chord state" is deferred to a dataset with silences.

## Limitations

- **Only maj/min classes.** The counts reduce every chord to its triad (G:7 -> G:maj, B:hdim7 -> B:min),
  as the vocabulary does. A seventh-chord vocabulary would need its own counts.
- **One weight for the whole prior.** tau and Laplace smoothing are the simplest controls; a prior
  mixed from several corpora, or conditioned on the key, was not tried.
- **Billboard is pop.** A jazz corpus (iReal Pro or JAAH, also in ChoCo) would fit the Jazz takes
  better, and would be one more choice fit to val.

## What the next version should look at

- **V4, learned emissions** (logistic regression or per-chord Gaussians on beat chroma): calibrated
  posteriors are what finding 3 says is missing. With them, the division by P(state) in
  `viterbi_discriminative` matters, a learned p_self becomes meaningful, and the Billboard prior should
  be retested at tau 1 (the code and counts are kept for that).
- **Beat tracking for syncopated styles** stays open from V2: Funk is still 33.8%.
