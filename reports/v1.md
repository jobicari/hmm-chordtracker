# V1: does averaging the chroma per beat help?

**Answer: it fixes flicker for frame-by-frame decisions, but adds nothing on top of Viterbi.** V1
changes one thing from V0: the 23 ms chroma frames are averaged over each detected beat before the
templates, so one decision (or one HMM step) is one beat. On the untouched test split this lifts
the per-decision argmax from 41.8% to 48.8% WCSR, while the HMM ends where V0's did: 53.3%
against 53.7%. V0's Viterbi was already pooling evidence over time, so fixing the segment
boundaries at the beats does the same job a different way.

| Test split, run once (60 takes) | V0 (frames) | V1 (beats) | changes / min, V0 → V1 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Argmax per decision (framewise) | 41.8 % | 48.8 % | 288 → 61 |
| **Viterbi** (p_self chosen on val) | **53.7 %** (0.95) | **53.3 %** (0.05) | 31 → 29 |

## Question

The roadmap's V1 question: *does pooling the chroma over beats fix arpeggios and flicker?* A guitar
part spreads a chord's notes over time (arpeggios, strums, muted "chucks"), so a single 23 ms frame
often holds one or two of the chord's notes. Averaging all the frames of a beat should put the
notes of the chord back together, and taking one decision per beat removes changes faster than a
beat by construction.

## Setup

Everything is V0 except the pooling step.

| | |
|---|---|
| Data, split, audio, features | as V0: GuitarSet comp takes, mono mic, the same song-level split; `chroma_cqt` hop 512 on the harmonic part |
| **Beats (new)** | `librosa.beat.beat_track`, hop 512, on the **full** mono audio (not the harmonic part) |
| **Pooling (new)** | mean of the chroma frames of each beat (`librosa.util.sync`); the stretch before the first beat and after the last one are segments too |
| Emission | as V0: 24 binary triad templates + N, softmax, beta 1 |
| Decoders | framewise argmax; `viterbi_discriminative` with `transition_loop(25, p_self)`, one step per beat |
| Back to time | each beat segment keeps its exact start and end time (`beats.segments_to_annotation`) |
| Metric | as V0: `mir_eval.chord.majmin`, duration-weighted, pooled; same label policy |
| Code | `configs/v1.yaml`, `src/chordtracker/beats.py`, `scripts/run_v1.py` |

The label policy is unchanged: on val, 10.0% of scored time is reduced to its triad and 4.8% is
excluded; on test 17.8% and 8.2%, as in V0.

## How the evaluation avoids leakage

Same rule as V0: **test is scored, never inspected.** Every choice below (beat source, mean or
median, p_self) was made on val. Test was run once, with the frozen code and p_self 0.05, and only
its pooled numbers appear here. No test take was plotted or examined.

## Before V1: the G#/A question from V0

V0 left one finding open: in val take `04_Funk2-119-G` (G minor), G# and A dominate the chroma.
`notebooks/V1_val_chroma_check.ipynb` answers it on val, using GuitarSet's transcription of every
played note:

- **Not tuning.** The players are within about 10 cents of A440, and librosa's tuning estimate
  agrees with the transcription within 8 cents on 55 of 60 val takes.
- **A body resonance.** A fixed peak at about **106 Hz**, between G#2 and A2, holds about 45% of
  the CQT energy of that take while notes sound, though a G#2 or A2 is played in only 3% of its
  frames. It stays at the same frequency in a take in another key. It is in every style (10% of the
  energy in SS, 38% in Funk), and takes with more of it score lower (correlation −0.65 over the 60
  val takes).
- **The triad templates are the bigger limit.** With a perfect chroma built from the transcribed
  notes, the V0 templates and Viterbi reach 70.8% on val (V0: 61.4%), and Funk only 48.8%:
  rootless, extended voicings contain a different triad (A#:maj7 played as F A D A# is D:min).

Removing the resonance was worth +2 to +3 points on val, but made about a third of the takes worse.
It is a feature change, so it was left for V2 and V1 stayed as planned.

## Choices on val

**Beats on the full signal.** The chroma uses the harmonic part, but HPSS removes the attacks a
beat tracker listens to. Against GuitarSet's annotated beats, the mean beat F-measure on val is
0.53 on the full signal and 0.19 on the harmonic part.

**Mean, not median.** Viterbi on val: 61.3% with the mean, 59.5% with the median.

**p_self.** One step is now a beat (about 0.5 s), so the V0 range does not carry over:

| System (val, beat mean) | WCSR | changes / min |
|---|---:|---:|
| framewise | 54.2 % | 62 |
| Viterbi 0.04 (= 1/25) | 54.2 % | 62 |
| **Viterbi 0.05** (chosen) | **61.3 %** | **29** |
| Viterbi 0.06 | 60.7 % | 24 |
| Viterbi 0.08 | 57.6 % | 20 |
| Viterbi 0.1 | 55.5 % | 18 |
| Viterbi 0.2 | 44.9 % | 10 |
| Viterbi 0.5 | 30.3 % | 4 |
| Viterbi 0.9 | 24.1 % | 1 |

- At 0.04 = 1/25 every transition is equally likely, and Viterbi gives exactly the framewise result.
  This is the same check as in V0, and the tests check it too.
- The best value sits just above 1/25, and anything from 0.2 up makes the path almost never change
  chord. Finding 2 explains why.

## Test, run once

| System | test WCSR | changes / min | takes improved | reduced | excluded |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| framewise (beats) | 48.8 % | 61 |  | 17.8 % | 8.2 % |
| Viterbi 0.05 (beats) | 53.3 % | 29 | 42 / 60 | 17.8 % | 8.2 % |

"Takes improved" counts the test takes where Viterbi beats framewise, as in V0. Compared with V0
on test: framewise +7.0 points, Viterbi −0.4 points. On val the same comparison gives +5.7 and
−0.1. This is reported, not investigated.

## Findings (all from val)

**1. Pooling replaces smoothing; it does not add to it.** Per decision, beats help a lot: framewise
goes from 48.5% (V0) to 54.2%, and from 282 to 62 changes a minute. With Viterbi, V1 and V0 tie,
61.3% against 61.4%, and V1 is better on 25 val takes and worse on 26. Per style, V1 is within
about 2 points of V0 everywhere (BN 53.9 vs 56.1, Funk 31.8 vs 29.1, Jazz 48.8 vs 49.2, Rock 75.9
vs 75.1, SS 84.1 vs 85.0).

**2. A beat average carries about 1/20 of the evidence, which is why p_self has to be so low.**
V0's Viterbi adds up the log-evidence of about 20 frames per beat. A beat mean puts those frames
into **one** column, so it carries about 1/20 as much, while the cost of a chord change,
log(p_self / ((1 − p_self) / 24)), stays the same. At p_self 0.5 that cost is log 24 ≈ 3.2, more
than a beat's evidence for a new chord usually is, so the path barely changes (4 per minute).
Summing the frames instead of averaging them gives the evidence back (sum = number of frames ×
mean, the segment-pooling identity in ROADMAP §3): with the sum, the usual range works again, and
p_self 0.8 gives 61.4%, the V0 score. A sharper softmax (a larger beta) would have the same effect.

**3. Beat errors cost about 2 points.** With GuitarSet's annotated beats in place of the tracker,
Viterbi reaches 63.0% on val. The tracker gets the tempo within 10% on 38 of 60 takes but often
puts the beats off the annotated grid.

**4. The emission is still the bottleneck.** The note oracle above (70.8% with perfect notes, 48.8%
on Funk) bounds what any chroma fix can do with triad templates, and V1's pooling does not move
the errors V0 finding 3 described.

## Limitations

- **Same vocabulary and emission as V0.** Triads only, binary templates, beta 1; between 10.0%
  (val) and 17.8% (test) of scored time is a richer chord compared as its triad.
- **Beats come from a generic tracker.** Funk syncopation and rubato put beats off the grid; the
  annotated-beat reference shows what that costs on val.
- **Offline.** `beat_track` and Viterbi both need the whole take. A live version needs causal beat
  tracking and fixed-lag decoding (V7).
- **One dataset**, as V0.

## What the next version should look at

The val evidence (finding 4 and the G#/A check) points at the features and the emission:

- **V2, better features:** remove the stationary body resonance (median subtraction or whitening),
  log compression, and a bass chroma to separate D:min from A#:maj7.
- **Then learned emissions** (V4) or seventh templates, against the note-oracle ceiling.
- Keep in mind finding 2: any change to how evidence is pooled changes the useful p_self range.

## Reproduce

```bash
python scripts/run_v1.py --split val                       # beat sweep, choose p_self (0.05)
python scripts/run_v1.py --split test --p-self 0.05        # once
```

`notebooks/V1_val_chroma_check.ipynb` reproduces the G#/A investigation on val (a few minutes; it
reloads all 60 val takes).
