# V7.3: frame-by-frame features

## Question

V7.1 and V7.2 compute the causal features on whole files; tests show that no column reads the future,
but a live system receives the recording one block of 2,048 samples (93 ms) at a time. V7.3 asks:
**can the features be computed block by block, give the same chords as the cached features, and keep
up with the music?** It is the last unbuilt piece between the research code and a prototype that plays
a recording to a musician with the chords on time. A small extra: **does a simple hold rule cut the
flicker at short delays as well as the fixed-lag Viterbi?**

Nothing is trained and nothing is scored on test: the features must match, not improve.

## Summary of the results

1. **Same chords.** On validation, chords from the block-by-block features score within 0.05 points of
   the cached features at V7.2's frozen points (0.28, 0.46 and 0.65 s of total look-ahead), with both
   seeds (0.001 to 0.044 points lower; the threshold was 0.1).
2. **Fast enough.** The whole live chain (features, CRNN, fixed-lag Viterbi) takes 7.3 ms per 93 ms
   block on the Mac's CPU (99th percentile 11 ms, real-time factor 0.08), flat over a 5.6-minute song.
   One block takes 0.31 s: the tuning estimate at 10 s, which a live system would run off the audio
   thread.
3. **The hold rule ties the fixed-lag Viterbi.** At the same delay neither beats the other with both
   seeds; the hold rule ends with slightly fewer chord changes (32 against 36 per minute at 0.65 s).
   No test run.

## Setup

`streaming_features.StreamingFeatures`: `step(block)` takes the next samples and returns every log-CQT
column that has become computable; `flush()` the rest at the end of the song. Each stage keeps only what
the past requires:

| Stage | Kept between blocks | Per block |
|---|---|---|
| Harmonic part | the STFT frames' magnitudes of the last 9 frames, the overlap-add buffer | 4 new STFT frames (hop 512, window 2,048), median over the past 9, frequency median, soft mask, overlap-add; harmonic sample n is final once the audio up to n + 2,048 has arrived |
| CQT | the harmonic audio (each bin reads at most its kernel, C2: 0.53 s, F#0: 1.06 s) | 168 inner products, each bin's exact kernel (`librosa.filters.wavelet`) centred where V7.1's delayed column puts it, scaled by sqrt(kernel length) as librosa does |
| Level + log | every CQT value so far, kept sorted | insert 168 values, read the 99th percentile, log(1 + 10 C / level) |
| Tuning | the first 10 s of audio | A440 until 10 s; then V7.1's estimate on those 10 s, from the same column |

**One difference from the cache.** librosa's CQT downsamples the low octaves before filtering; the
block-by-block CQT computes each kernel at full rate. Their values differ by 1 to 3 % in the lowest
octaves (on synthetic tones; 0.03 % in the top octave), and in the first frames of a song, where the
running level rests on a handful of near-zero values. `streaming_features.offline_log_cqt` runs the
same direct computation on a whole file: the block-by-block columns equal it (largest difference
2e-6), and the end-to-end check below uses it to decode the 80 validation songs.

Tests (`tests/test_streaming_features.py`): block by block equals the whole-file direct computation on
synthetic audio (shorter and longer than the 10 s tuning point, any block size) and on 6 real training
songs on both grids (`-m slow`); each column comes out at most one frame after its frame; the harmonic
part equals V7.1's; the columns stay within 0.01 (mean) of the cache away from the first frames.
`tests/test_hold.py` checks the hold rule.

## Results on validation

**Same chords** (`scripts/v7_3_check.py`): aligned WCSR %, mean of RWC-P and GuitarSet.

| total look-ahead | point (V7.2, frozen) | seed | cached features | block by block | difference |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.28 s | C2, input look-ahead 2, network alone | 0 | 76.17 | 76.17 | -0.001 |
| | | 1 | 75.78 | 75.76 | -0.013 |
| 0.46 s | F#0, input look-ahead 2, fixed-lag lag 2 | 0 | 77.17 | 77.14 | -0.025 |
| | | 1 | 75.87 | 75.86 | -0.012 |
| 0.65 s | F#0, input look-ahead 2, fixed-lag lag 4 | 0 | 77.86 | 77.83 | -0.023 |
| | | 1 | 76.33 | 76.29 | -0.044 |

**Speed** (`scripts/v7_3_speed.py`, the longest RWC-P validation song, 335 s, 3,608 blocks; C2 grid,
input look-ahead 2, fixed-lag Viterbi lag 4; Mac CPU), ms per block:

| stage | mean | 99th percentile | max | mean, last minute |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| features | 4.47 | 7.26 | 310.8 (tuning at 10 s) | 4.65 |
| CRNN | 2.73 | 5.23 | 18.7 | 2.88 |
| decoder | 0.06 | 0.10 | 1.0 | 0.06 |
| **whole chain** | **7.26** | **11.25** | 313.7 | 7.59 |

The block budget is 92.9 ms. A first version that grew its buffers by copying them took 43 ms per
block by the end of the same song and would have fallen behind on songs of about 12 minutes; the buffers now grow
in amortized constant time.

**The hold rule** (`scripts/v7_3_hold.py`, `hold.py`): keep the current chord until a new one has been
the network's most likely chord for m frames in a row. It decides frame t at t + m - 1, so it costs
m - 1 frames of total look-ahead, like a decoder lag. On V7.2's 0.28 s network (C2, input look-ahead 2),
aligned WCSR % and chord changes per minute, seed 0 / seed 1:

| total look-ahead | hold rule | fixed-lag Viterbi (same delay) |
|---:|---|---|
| 0.28 s | m = 1 (network alone): 76.17 / 75.78, 58 / 55 per min | |
| 0.37 s | m = 2: 76.34 / 75.96, 45 / 43 | lag 1: 76.67 / 75.29, 44 / 43 |
| 0.46 s | m = 3: 76.69 / 76.22, 39 / 38 | lag 2: 77.10 / 75.70, 38 / 40 |
| 0.65 s | m = 5: 76.77 / 76.41, 32 / 33 | lag 4: 77.68 / 76.31, 36 / 37 |

The fixed-lag Viterbi is ahead with seed 0 and behind with seed 1 at every delay; the two-seed rule is
not met either way.

## Findings (all from validation)

**1. The research numbers hold for a live system.** The block-by-block chain gives the chords of the
cached runs to within 0.05 points, so every V7.1 and V7.2 result describes what the live chain would do.
The small CQT difference (librosa's downsampling) does not reach the chords.

**2. Real time is not a constraint.** 7 ms of the 93 ms per block, and flat over the song. The only
spike is the one-off tuning estimate at 10 s (0.31 s), which needs its own thread or an incremental
estimate in an application.

**3. Either smoother works.** The hold rule and the fixed-lag Viterbi give the same accuracy at the same
delay, within the seed noise. The hold rule needs no transition matrix and ends with a few fewer
changes per minute; the fixed-lag Viterbi stays the frozen choice from V7.2.

## Limitations

- **The CQT is not librosa's** in the low octaves (1 to 3 %); the effect on chords was measured on
  validation only, at three points, and is within 0.05 points.
- **Timing on one machine** (the Mac's CPU, no GPU), Python and NumPy; an application in another
  language would need its own measurement.
- **The tuning estimate** blocks for 0.31 s once.
- **The hold rule** was scored on one network (the 0.28 s point), two seeds.

## What the next version should look at

- **A prototype for listening**: the live chain with delayed playback, the playback delay and the lead
  time set by ear with a musician; the chain is ready for it.
- **The N class and sevenths**, still open since V5, on the C2 grid with 2 frames of input look-ahead.
