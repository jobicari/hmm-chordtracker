# V5: a CNN acoustic model on the log-CQT, with V4's decoder

**Answer: yes, a learned acoustic model is the step that pays.** V5 replaces V4's logistic regression
on 24 beat chroma numbers with a small convolutional network that reads 1.4 s of log-CQT around every
frame, trained on two datasets (RWC-P pop songs and GuitarSet comp takes) with pitch-shift
augmentation. The decoder is V4's, unchanged. On val, against V4 refitted on exactly the same training
data, V5 gains **+8.6 points on RWC-P** and **+3.9 on GuitarSet**. On GuitarSet test, measured once, it
scores **72.9% WCSR** (V4: 62.3%).

| Val, p_self 0.4 (chosen on the mean of the two sets) | RWC-P (20 songs) | GuitarSet (60 takes) | mean |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| V4 as frozen (trained on GuitarSet train), best p_self | 68.52 % | 70.49 % | |
| V4 refitted on RWC-P train only, best p_self | 70.11 % | | |
| V4 refitted on V5's training data (RWC-P + GuitarSet train) | 69.91 % | 71.53 % | 70.72 % |
| **V5: CNN acoustic model + V4 decoder** | **78.55 %** | **75.41 %** | **76.98 %** |

| Test, run once, p_self 0.4 | WCSR | changes / min |
|---|---:|---:|
| GuitarSet (60 takes), V4 (p_self 0.5) | 62.31 % | 25.4 |
| **GuitarSet (60 takes), V5** | **72.89 %** | 25.5 |
| **RWC-P (20 songs), V5** | **78.95 %** | 28.2 |

## Question

V4 showed that learned weights plus a calibrated posterior let the Billboard change prior work at full
strength. Refitting it on 60 RWC-P songs, three times more data than GuitarSet train, added only 1.6
points: a linear model of one beat's 24 chroma numbers had saturated. The roadmap's V5 asks whether
learned *features* help: a small CNN that sees the spectrum itself (octave, bass note, a 1.4 s window)
instead of 12 folded pitch classes.

## Data

GuitarSet alone is 15 progressions, too few to train a network. V5 adds **RWC Popular Music (RWC-P)**:
100 Japanese pop songs (6.8 h, 34 artists), audio and chord labels under CC BY-NC 4.0
(Zenodo 18656623; labels from rwc-music/rwc-annotations). The labels were checked on val before use:
aligned with the audio (94 of 100 songs best within ±0.3 s), none transposed.

- **RWC-P split** (`splits/rwc_p.json`): 60 / 20 / 20 songs, grouped by artist so no artist is in two
  splits, seed 0.
- **GuitarSet**: the comp takes and the 60 / 60 / 60 split by progression that V0 to V4 used.
- **Training** on RWC-P train + GuitarSet train: 120 songs, **163,422 frames** with a target.
- **Two val and two test sets**, scored separately and never pooled: a pop song and a solo guitar
  take are different tasks, and pooling would let RWC-P's longer songs dominate.

## Setup

    input     log-CQT, 24 bins per octave from F#0, hop 2048 (93 ms), V2's compression:
              divide by the take's 99th percentile, then log(1 + 10 C); harmonic part of the audio
    patch     144 bins (C1..B6) x 15 frames (1.4 s), centred on the frame it labels, zeros past the edges
    target    24 classes: maj/min read from the chord's third (C:maj7 -> C:maj, B:dim -> B:min);
              chords with no third (sus, power chords), N and X have no target and are left out
    network   conv 16, conv 16, max-pool pitch 2 (144 -> 72 rows, one per semitone)
              conv 32, conv 32, max-pool time 3 (15 -> 5 frames)
              conv 64, mean over time, dropout 0.5, dense 4,608 -> 24
              every conv 3 x 3 + batch norm + ReLU; 145,800 weights, 110,616 in the dense layer
    training  cross-entropy, Adam lr 1e-3, batch 256, random shift k in -5..+6 semitones per example
              (crop rows 12 - 2k of a 168-bin CQT, label root + k), early stopping on the mean val
              frame accuracy (patience 5), seed 0
    decoder   V4's: frame posteriors -> beats (geometric mean of the frames in each beat) ->
              viterbi_discriminative(P, A, p_state = prior), A = p_self I + (1 - p_self) Q

Symbols: A is the 24 x 24 transition matrix, A[i, j] = P(chord j at the next beat | chord i now); I is
the identity matrix, so the diagonal of A is p_self, the probability of staying on the same chord one
more beat; Q is V3's Billboard change prior (zero diagonal, transposition-invariant counts from 730
songs, tau 1). prior is the frequency of each class among the training targets, counted in all 12 keys,
because training sees every frame at a random shift.

- **Pitch shift instead of transposing chroma.** The CQT is computed 6 semitones wider than the
  network's input on each side. Shifting the music up k semitones is cutting the 144 rows 2k bins
  lower, so a C:maj frame read at k = +2 looks exactly like a D:maj frame. Patches are cut on the fly
  from the cached CQT (`data/processed/v5_logcqt/`), never stored: a frame sits in 15 overlapping
  patches at 12 shifts.
- **Pitch is pooled only to one row per semitone**, and the dense layer sees all 72 rows, so the
  network knows where a pattern is (which root, which octave), not only that it exists.
- **Beats come from V1's tracker**, as in V4, so V4 and V5 decode and score exactly the same segments.

Code: `src/chordtracker/features_v5.py` (log-CQT and cache), `data_v5.py` (songs, targets, patches),
`model_v5.py` (the CNN), `train_v5.py` (training with early stopping), `acoustic_hmm.py` (frames to
beats, training prior, decoder); `configs/v5.yaml` (extends v2.yaml); `scripts/run_v5.py` (trains and
saves `results/v5/model.pt` on the val run, the test run loads it); `scripts/run_v5_baseline.py` (V4
refitted on V5's training data); one test file per module.

## How the evaluation avoids leakage

Same rule as V0 to V4: **test is scored, never inspected.** RWC-P is split by artist and GuitarSet by
progression, so neither val nor test shares an artist or a lead sheet with train. The network's
weights see train only. Every choice (architecture, input, epochs through early stopping, p_self) was
made on val. `data_v5.rwc_takes` refuses to load RWC-P test; only the frozen run script can. Test ran
once, after the freeze, and only its summary numbers were read.

## Findings (all from val)

**1. The gain is the acoustic model, not the data.** Given both datasets, V4's logistic regression
barely moves: RWC-P 70.15% at best, against 70.11% trained on RWC-P alone. The CNN on the same data
adds 8.6 points on RWC-P and 3.9 on GuitarSet.

**2. It helps most where chroma loses information.** Funk gains 13.6 points over the V4 refit and Jazz
4.9 (rootless, muted and seventh voicings, where the octave and the bass note matter), BN 3.1; Rock and
SS, already plain triads, stay level. On RWC-P, 18 of 20 songs improve, and the hardest one (RWC_P031,
23% for V4 as frozen) reaches 45%.

**3. It learns fast and then overfits.** Val accuracy is 74% after one epoch and peaks at epoch 5
while the training loss keeps falling (0.39 -> 0.29 by epoch 10): 145k weights on 163k frames, most of
them in the dense layer. Early stopping keeps epoch 5.

**4. The decoder carries over.** Viterbi WCSR is at or above frame accuracy, and the p_self curve is as
flat as V4's (76.3 to 77.0% mean across 0.2 to 0.8). A one-epoch dry run on four songs had shown the
opposite risk: with near-flat posteriors, dividing by a 68%-major prior made Viterbi hold one minor
chord for a whole song. A trained model's posteriors are sharp enough that this does not happen.

## Test

Run once, after the freeze, with the saved model and p_self 0.4.

| | WCSR | changes / min | scored s | of which reduced s | excluded s |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| GuitarSet (60 takes) | **72.89 %** | 25.5 | 1,723.3 | 306.3 | 153.1 |
| RWC-P (20 songs) | **78.95 %** | 28.2 | 4,783.4 | 1,851.2 | 248.1 |

GuitarSet test scores exactly the same seconds as V1 to V4, so the comparison is direct: **+10.6 points
over V4** (62.31%). The val-test gap on GuitarSet is 2.5 points (V4: 8.2, V2: 9.8); on RWC-P test is
0.4 points above val.

## Limitations

- **Only maj/min, no N.** Sevenths are reduced to triads (39% of RWC-P's scored test time is reduced),
  and the network has no no-chord class: N frames are left out of training and always decoded as a
  chord.
- **Two small datasets.** 120 training songs, and every choice was made on 20 RWC-P songs and 60
  GuitarSet takes. Overfitting starts at epoch 5.
- **Funk is still the weakest style** (51% on val), even if V5 gains the most there.
- **Not bit-exact on the Mac's GPU.** Seeds fix the initialization, shuffling and shifts, but some MPS
  kernels are not deterministic; a retrain can differ in the last digits.

## What the next version should look at

- **The N class** (a separate experiment): RWC-P has N at song starts and ends; GuitarSet has
  none.
- **Sevenths**: the log-CQT keeps every note, so a CNN can be trained on them; the decoder would need
  its own change counts for them.
- **Regularization** (weight decay, a smaller dense layer, more augmentation) to push the epoch-5
  overfitting later, and more training data (AAM, synthetic and CC BY, is licensed for it).
- **Funk**: beat tracking on syncopated takes.
