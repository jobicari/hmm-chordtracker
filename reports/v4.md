# V4: learned emissions, and a chord-change prior at full strength

**Answer: yes, and they need each other.** V4 replaces the hand-made template score with logistic
regression fitted on the train split, on the same 24 beat numbers V2 computes. Alone, it adds about
one point on val. Its real effect is calibration: its posteriors behave like probabilities, so the
Billboard chord-change prior that V3 could only use at a quarter of its strength now helps at full

## Question

Since V0 the evidence for chord c on a beat has been a template fit score, softmaxed with sharpness 1.
V3 found that these numbers are not probabilities: two plausible chords differ by about one nat per
beat, so a prior counted on real songs (5.3 nats between a common and a rare move) overrides five
beats of evidence. The roadmap's V4 asks whether learned emissions beat hand-made ones, and whether
calibrated ones let the decoder use its prior and self-loop at their true strength.

## Setup

Features, beats and decoder algorithm are V2's (`configs/v2.yaml`, which `configs/v4.yaml` extends).
Only the emission and the transition matrix change.

    x         = [treble chroma (12), bass chroma (12)]          the V2 beat means, unchanged
    P(c | x)  = softmax(W x + b)                                 W (24 x 24), b (24): logistic regression
    decoder   = viterbi_discriminative(P, A, p_state = prior)    scores P(c | x) / prior(c)
    A         = p_self I + (1 - p_self) Q                        Q: V3's Billboard change prior, tau 1

Symbols: x is one beat's 24 inputs; c is one of the 24 maj/min chords; W and b are the learned
weights; prior(c) is the frequency of chord c among the training rows; A is the transition matrix,
A[i, j] = P(chord j at the next beat | chord i at this beat); I is the identity matrix, so the
diagonal of A is p_self, the probability of staying on the same chord; Q is the V3 change prior, with
a zero diagonal, giving where a change goes (transposition-invariant counts from 730 Billboard songs,
Laplace smoothing, tau = 1 = the counted frequencies).

- **Training rows.** Each train beat gets the lead-sheet chord that covers most of it, reduced to its
  maj/min class. 3,294 labelled beats, each used in all 12 transpositions (chroma and label rolled
  together): 39,528 rows. Without transposition the model would learn train's keys.
- **Regularization.** L2, C = 10 (C = 1: 67.72%, C = 100: 67.96% on val, flat loop).
- **The division by P(state).** Train is 92% major (val about 70%). Logistic regression learns that
  as part of P(c | x); dividing by prior(c) turns the posterior into a scaled likelihood
  P(x | c) / P(x), which leaves the class balance to the decoder. This is the roadmap's "the P(state)
  division now matters".
- **24 states, no N.** GuitarSet's labels contain no N, so there is nothing to learn it from (V2 never
  decoded N on val either).

Code: `src/chordtracker/learned_emission.py` (plain functions), `configs/v4.yaml`, `scripts/run_v4.py`
(fits on train and saves `results/v4/model.json` on the val run; the test run loads it and never
refits), `tests/test_learned_emission.py`. Ablations: `scripts/v4_cache_beats.py`,
`scripts/v4_ablate_emissions.py`, `scripts/v4_ablate_priors.py`; every val row in `results/v4/ablations/`
(the class-balanced row is in `calibration_control.json`, from a one-off check not kept as a script).

## How the evaluation avoids leakage

Same rule as V0 to V3: **test is scored, never inspected.** The split is by progression (train = 1,
val = 3, test = 2 of each style), so the emission is fitted on lead sheets that neither val nor test
plays. Train is the only split the weights see. Every choice (model family, C, the division,
tau, p_self) was made on val, one change at a time, with p_self re-swept for each. Billboard shares no
song with GuitarSet. The "val labels" prior is a ceiling, never a candidate. Test ran once, after the
freeze, and only its summary numbers were read.

## Findings (all from val)

**1. The division by P(state) is worth 9 points.** Without it logistic regression scores 59.1%: train
is 92% major, so the model's posteriors carry a "say major" habit that val (30% minor) punishes.
Divided by the train prior, 68.4%. Fitting with balanced classes does the same job less well (66.5%).

**2. Learned weights help where templates are wrong.** Against V2, Funk and Jazz gain about 5 points
each (sevenths, rootless and muted voicings, where a binary triad template misleads) and Rock and SS
lose (plain triads, where the template is already the right model). Alone that is +1.1 points, 30 / 29
takes: within noise on 60 takes.

**3. Calibration turns the prior round.** On V2's evidence every Billboard tau above 0.25 hurts
(tau 1: 60.9%). On logistic-regression evidence WCSR rises with tau to 1 and then levels off (69.6,
70.2, 70.7, 70.6% at tau 0.25, 0.5, 1, 1.5): the prior can now be used at the strength the counts say.
Against the same emission with the flat loop, Billboard improves 32 takes and worsens 17, and every
style gains.

**4. Calibration alone is not enough.** One number, beta 4.85 fitted on train, calibrates the
templates as well (log-loss 1.55 vs 1.65) at V2's accuracy, and also moves p_self from 0.06 to 0.30.
But Billboard adds only 0.5 points to those templates. The learned weights and the prior together
are what gain.

**5. p_self moves towards the lead sheets.** The best p_self rises from 0.06 (V2) to 0.4 to 0.5. The
train lead sheets imply 0.84 per beat (6.4 beats per chord); 0.84 is not the best value on any row,
so the evidence still claims a little more than it carries, but it is the same order of magnitude.

**6. Billboard over GuitarSet's own train lead sheets.** The train prior at tau 1.5 ties (70.84%)
but gets its gain from Rock and BN, whose train progressions resemble val's, and costs Jazz 9 points
(train has no ii -> V). Billboard is the prior that generalizes, which fits the aim of a tracker for
any music.

## Test

Run once, after the freeze, with the saved train model and p_self 0.5: **62.31% WCSR**, 25.4 changes
per minute; 1,723.3 s scored, of which 306.3 s reduced and 153.1 s excluded, the same as V1 and V2.
That is +4.8 points over V2's 57.53%. The val-test gap is 8.2 points (V2: 9.8, V1: 8.0).

## Limitations

- **Train is five lead sheets.** 3,294 beats from 5 progressions, 92% major: the weights have seen few
  chord contexts, and every choice was made on 60 val takes, where 30 / 29 is noise.
- **Only maj/min, no N.** The learned model reduces sevenths to triads like the templates, and has no
  no-chord class because GuitarSet has no N.
- **One beat at a time.** The model sees one beat's 24 numbers; neighbouring beats enter only through
  the decoder.
- **Cloud vs Mac.** The ablations ran in a cloud copy, about 0.3 points above the Mac on the same
  settings; the plateau and every choice agree.

## What the next version should look at

- **V5, a small CNN on log-CQT patches** (roadmap): learned features as well as learned weights, with
  pitch-shift augmentation in place of chroma rolling. The V4 decoder (division by the train prior,
  Billboard prior at full strength) carries over unchanged.
- **Funk is still the weakest style** (41.1% on val): beat tracking for syncopated takes stays open.
- **A larger vocabulary** (sevenths) is now possible on the emission side; it would need its own
  change counts.
